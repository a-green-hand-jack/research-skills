# Communication and Project Health

Collaboration health is easier to preserve through frequent small alignment than through rare crisis meetings.

## Communicate before a delay becomes a surprise

If a commitment will slip, report it early with:

- what changed,
- what is blocked,
- revised timing,
- impact on dependent tasks,
- help needed.

Silence transfers planning risk to collaborators.

## Use the right communication mode

Use asynchronous channels for durable updates, decisions, files, and nonurgent questions.

Use synchronous discussion for:

- scientific disagreement,
- ambiguous requirements,
- complex interpretation,
- emotionally sensitive issues,
- decisions with many interacting constraints.

After important conversations, record decisions in a shared written form.

## Share artifacts, not just conclusions

Where appropriate, collaborators should have access to relevant:

- data,
- protocols,
- code,
- analysis outputs,
- manuscripts,
- decision records.

Trust is difficult to maintain if participants can only see polished conclusions from each other's work.

## Run lightweight health checks

Periodically ask:

- Are the scientific goals still aligned?
- Are responsibilities clear?
- Is anyone blocked?
- Have contributions changed enough to revisit authorship?
- Has the scope expanded without resources?
- Are there unresolved disagreements being avoided?

## Keep successful collaborations alive

A proven working relationship has accumulated knowledge about communication styles, strengths, weaknesses, and standards. When the scientific questions remain worthwhile, preserving a productive collaboration can reduce coordination cost in future projects.
