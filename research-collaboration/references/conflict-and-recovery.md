# Conflict and Recovery

Do not treat all collaboration problems as interpersonal conflict. First identify the failure mode.

## Diagnose the problem

Common categories:

- **scientific:** disagreement about evidence, interpretation, or method,
- **scope:** project or tasks changed without shared agreement,
- **operational:** missed deadlines, blocked dependencies, poor handoffs,
- **credit:** authorship or acknowledgment expectations diverged,
- **communication:** decisions are implicit, delayed, or differently understood,
- **incentives:** the project has become low priority for one participant,
- **trust:** materials, decisions, or contributions are being withheld or misrepresented.

Different categories require different remedies.

## Address the smallest resolvable unit

Instead of "the collaboration is failing," state a concrete problem:

> Analysis A has been blocked for three weeks because dataset B has no delivery date.

Then agree on a specific recovery action.

## Give repair a real chance

A missed commitment may have legitimate causes. Re-establish expectations explicitly and observe whether behavior changes.

If repeated attempts do not restore a workable project, reduce dependencies and define a clean exit rather than allowing indefinite resentment and blockage.

## Seek neutral advice

When the conflict involves power imbalance, authorship, or ambiguous norms, consult an experienced scientist, mentor, ombudsperson, or relevant institutional process as appropriate.

## Preserve scientific integrity during exit

Even when a collaboration ends:

- do not erase legitimate contributions,
- respect data/material agreements,
- document artifact ownership and access,
- clarify what work can continue and by whom,
- resolve authorship on work already performed as fairly as possible.
