# Data Management

A data-management plan should describe the life cycle of research data from acquisition to long-term disposition.

## Plan before collection

At minimum define:

- what data will be created or acquired,
- formats and naming conventions,
- directory/project structure,
- metadata and data dictionary requirements,
- quality-control procedures,
- storage location and backup,
- access control,
- privacy/security constraints,
- retention and archival plan,
- sharing plan,
- responsibilities for maintaining the data.

## Check sponsor and policy requirements early

Funders, institutions, journals, and data providers may impose different requirements. Determine them before building the project around an incompatible storage or sharing assumption.

## Make data understandable

A file is not reusable merely because it exists.

Provide enough metadata to answer:

- What does each field mean?
- What are the units and coding conventions?
- How were missing values represented?
- How were data generated and processed?
- Which version or collection date is this?
- What license or access condition applies?

## Design for continuity

Avoid data organization that only one person understands. Use stable conventions and documentation so a collaborator can take over without reconstructing the project from private memory.

## Plan restricted data explicitly

Open science does not require violating privacy, consent, contractual terms, or legal constraints.

When data cannot be public, document:

- why access is restricted,
- who can request access,
- what review or agreement is needed,
- which metadata can remain public,
- whether synthetic or de-identified alternatives are appropriate.
