# Peer-Review Ethics and Confidentiality

## Confirm fitness to review

Before accepting, check:

- relevant expertise,
- enough time to complete a careful review,
- conflicts of interest,
- venue-specific reviewer requirements.

If expertise covers only one part of a multidisciplinary paper, tell the editor rather than pretending to cover the whole manuscript.

## Treat manuscripts as privileged material

Do not disclose, reuse, or exploit unpublished ideas, data, or methods obtained through peer review.

Do not share the review copy with others unless the venue or editor explicitly permits it.

## Tool and AI use

Reviewer confidentiality rules apply to external tools. Do not send manuscript text, figures, data, or confidential review material to an AI system or other third-party service unless the journal/conference policy explicitly allows it and the confidentiality model is compatible.

When policy is unclear, do not assume permission.

## Manage conflicts and bias

Declare conflicts rather than trying to self-correct silently.

Evaluate the work independently of author affiliation, nationality, prestige, seniority, or personal relationships. Review the scientific evidence, not assumptions about the people who produced it.

## Use private editor comments appropriately

Communicate sensitive concerns to the editor when needed, such as:

- suspected conflict of interest,
- ethical concerns,
- serious reviewer misconduct in a revision cycle,
- reasons for a recommendation that should not be framed as an author-facing demand.

Do not put a major scientific objection only in private comments; authors need enough information to address problems in the work.
