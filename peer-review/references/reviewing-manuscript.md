# Reviewing a Manuscript

## Read in passes

### Pass 1 — orientation

Identify:

- the claimed contribution,
- the research question,
- the basic design,
- the main figures,
- the intended scope.

Do not start by line-editing prose.

### Pass 2 — scientific audit

Ask:

- Do the experiments or analyses answer the stated question?
- Are the main claims supported by the presented evidence?
- Are alternative explanations or confounders addressed?
- Are statistical methods appropriate for the design and dependencies?
- Are figures and tables consistent with the text?
- Is methodology described well enough to evaluate or reproduce the work?
- Are limitations material to the claims acknowledged?

### Pass 3 — coherence and completeness

Check the supplement, appendices, data/code statements, and any material needed to support the conclusions. The main paper should remain understandable without requiring the reader to discover essential logic hidden in supplementary files.

## Review every figure

For each figure:

- identify the claimed takeaway,
- verify labels, units, groups, sample sizes, and uncertainty,
- check that the visual supports the textual claim,
- look for unexplained exclusions or transformations,
- verify that caption and main text do not disagree.

## Consider broader context

Check whether the work engages the relevant literature and whether novelty or significance claims are proportionate.

Suggest missing citations only when they materially improve scientific context. Do not use review to inflate citation counts or require citation of the reviewer's own work.

## Distinguish "I would do it differently" from "the inference is not supported"

A reviewer should not demand an entirely different project unless the current design cannot support its central claims.

When proposing additional work, state what uncertainty the requested experiment or analysis would resolve.

## Re-reviewing a revision

Evaluate whether the authors resolved the scientific concern, not whether they followed your preferred implementation. A different solution is acceptable if it addresses the underlying issue.
