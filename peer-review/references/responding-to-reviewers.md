# Responding to Reviewers

The response document is part of the scientific revision, not a cover note. Make it easy for reviewers to verify what changed and why.

## Start with an overview

Open with a concise summary of major changes, especially new experiments, analyses, scope changes, or corrections that address central concerns.

Then reproduce or clearly identify reviewer comments and place each response immediately after the relevant comment.

## Answer every point

Do not skip difficult comments. If one bullet contains multiple questions, split them and answer each explicitly.

A useful response pattern:

1. direct answer,
2. what changed,
3. evidence or reasoning,
4. exact location in the revised manuscript.

## Lead with the answer

Do not make the reviewer read a page of background before discovering whether you agree.

Examples:

- "We agree and have repeated the analysis using..."
- "We agree that the original wording overstated causality and have revised..."
- "We respectfully disagree because the proposed analysis would estimate a different target quantity; however, we have added..."

## Make the response self-contained

Quote short revised passages or describe the concrete change and provide line/section references. Clearly distinguish text that was already present from text newly added.

## Treat misunderstanding as information

If a reviewer misunderstood a point, first ask whether the manuscript allowed that misunderstanding. Even when the original text seems technically correct, clearer wording may improve the paper for future readers.

## Disagree scientifically, not emotionally

When declining a request:

- acknowledge the concern,
- state the underlying scientific issue,
- explain why the requested change is not appropriate or feasible,
- provide an alternative resolution when possible,
- involve the editor when reviewer requests conflict with each other or with journal policy.

## Separate the private draft from the final response

If reactions are emotionally charged, write an internal version first to analyze the critiques. The submitted response should be a separate professional document optimized for clarity and resolution.
