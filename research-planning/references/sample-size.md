# Sample Size and Precision

Sample size is a design decision about what uncertainty and error rates are acceptable for the scientific question. It is not a generic request for "more data."

## Define what the sample size must achieve

Depending on the study, the target may be based on:

- power for a prespecified effect,
- desired confidence/credible interval width,
- precision of an estimate,
- expected event counts,
- prediction performance and validation stability,
- ability to detect a practically meaningful effect.

State the target explicitly.

## Justify assumptions

A calculation is only as good as assumptions about:

- effect size,
- variability,
- event rate,
- correlation or clustering,
- attrition or missingness,
- multiple testing,
- model complexity.

Use realistic or conservative values, preferably informed by prior data rather than best-case scenarios.

## Avoid post hoc rationalization

"We used the samples available" may describe feasibility but does not establish that the study can answer the intended question.

When sample size is constrained, shift the claim accordingly. A small study may be appropriate for feasibility, estimation, or exploration even when it cannot support a strong confirmatory conclusion.

## Low power has two problems

Low power does not only increase false negatives. Among findings that cross a significance threshold, small studies can preferentially surface exaggerated effect estimates.

Therefore, do not interpret a large observed effect in a small study as automatically compensating for limited sample size.

## Plan for missingness and exclusions

If the required analyzable sample is N, the recruitment or acquisition target may need to be larger. Prespecify exclusion criteria so that sample size does not become a result-dependent decision.

## Seek statistical input early

Complex dependence, adaptive designs, rare outcomes, high-dimensional models, and nonstandard estimands often require design-specific reasoning. Statistical consultation is most valuable before data collection, when the design can still change.
