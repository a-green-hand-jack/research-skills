# Protocol and Preregistration

A protocol turns the research question into a record of what will be done before outcomes can influence decisions.

## What to prespecify for confirmatory work

As applicable, document:

- research question and hypothesis,
- study population or system,
- inclusion/exclusion rules,
- primary and secondary outcomes,
- target sample size and rationale,
- data collection procedures,
- preprocessing and transformations,
- statistical model or primary comparison,
- handling of missing data,
- exclusion and quality-control rules,
- multiplicity strategy,
- stopping rules,
- planned sensitivity analyses.

The goal is not bureaucracy. It is to reduce the ability of outcomes to silently rewrite the original question.

## Distinguish deviations from misconduct

Real projects change. A deviation from plan is not inherently a problem if it is documented and justified.

When plans change:

1. record what changed,
2. record when it changed relative to seeing outcomes,
3. explain why,
4. distinguish prespecified from post hoc analyses in reporting.

## Do not force exploration into preregistration theater

Exploratory projects may not support detailed prior hypotheses. In that case, document goals, data provenance, analysis workflow, and the boundary between exploration and later confirmatory testing.

The problem is not exploration; the problem is presenting an outcome-discovered hypothesis as if it were independently predicted.

## Registered reports

When appropriate and available, registered reports can move peer review of the question and method before the result is known. This aligns publication incentives more closely with design quality than with whether the outcome is positive.

## Agent checklist

When reviewing a protocol, check whether a future analyst could infer:

- what the primary question was,
- which decisions were fixed before results,
- which outcomes matter most,
- what would count as evidence for or against the hypothesis,
- which choices remain flexible and how that flexibility will be reported.
