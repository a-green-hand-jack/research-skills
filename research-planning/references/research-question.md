# Research Question

A successful project begins with a question that is important enough to matter and precise enough to determine what evidence is needed.

## Separate topic from question

Topic:

> microbiome and treatment response

Question:

> Among patients receiving treatment X, is baseline microbial diversity associated with response at time Y after adjusting for prespecified clinical covariates?

The question should constrain the population/system, quantities or phenomena of interest, comparison, and intended inference.

## Establish why the question is unresolved

Use prior literature systematically enough to know whether the question is genuinely open. A new study is weakly motivated if its novelty depends on overlooking older work or an adjacent field.

A useful gap statement explains both:

- what is not known,
- why resolving it changes scientific understanding or decisions.

## Match precision to study mode

**Confirmatory research** requires a sufficiently defined question and hypothesis to prespecify primary outcomes, key comparisons, and analysis decisions.

**Exploratory research** can begin with broader aims, but it should be reported honestly as exploration rather than retrofitted into a confirmatory narrative after patterns are seen.

Both modes are scientifically valuable; confusing them creates misleading certainty.

## Translate the question into observable evidence

For each concept in the question, identify:

- how it will be measured,
- the unit of analysis,
- relevant timing,
- inclusion/exclusion boundaries,
- what comparison or model would answer the question,
- what result would change your belief.

If a key concept has no defensible operational definition, the question is not yet ready for study design.

## Stress-test importance

Ask:

- If the answer were known, what would change?
- Is the study distinguishing plausible alternatives or only adding another estimate?
- Does the question matter to the relevant scientific or stakeholder community?
- Is the expected evidence capable of supporting the intended conclusion?
