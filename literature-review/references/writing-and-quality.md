# Writing and Quality Control for a Literature Review

A review should leave the reader with a model of the field: major achievements, active disagreements, limitations of current evidence, and the most important unresolved questions.

## Build a conceptual structure

Before full prose, draw or outline the relationships among themes. Possible organizing principles include:

- mechanism,
- competing theories,
- chronology of a scientific problem,
- methods and their tradeoffs,
- population or context,
- stages in a process,
- evidence for and against a claim.

Use chronology only when historical sequence explains scientific development.

## Write paragraphs as synthesis units

Each paragraph should have:

1. a claim about the literature,
2. evidence from multiple relevant studies,
3. explanation of agreement or disagreement,
4. a conclusion or transition that changes the reader's state of understanding.

Avoid turning citations into a list of paper summaries.

## Be critical without becoming performative

Critical review means evaluating the strength and limitations of evidence, not maximizing negative language.

Discuss:

- what has been established,
- what remains uncertain,
- why some evidence is more persuasive,
- which methodological weaknesses recur,
- what would discriminate between competing explanations.

## Treat your own work symmetrically

If your work belongs in the review, include it according to the same relevance and evidence criteria used for other studies. Avoid both promotional overemphasis and artificial omission.

## Seek diverse feedback

Fresh readers can identify unclear structure, hidden assumptions, missing communities of literature, and overconfident conclusions. Conflicting feedback can itself reveal where the field lacks consensus.

## Final quality check

The review should answer:

- What are the field's major established findings?
- Which conclusions have the strongest evidence?
- Where do findings disagree, and why might they disagree?
- What are recurring methodological limitations?
- What questions remain important and genuinely unresolved?
- Is the search current enough to support those statements?
